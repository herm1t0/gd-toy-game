# Presentation reads at a fixed scale, from decals, not paint

Presentation (#9) commits to a **fixed camera** and **decal-based identity**. The camera is perspective, FOV 45° at a 60° pitch, pinned to the Lane centreline at ~48 px/m so the whole 40 m Lane width is on screen, and panning along the axis is its only degree of freedom — no zoom, no yaw, no rotation. Sides are never told apart by recolouring models: a 0.9 m unshaded ring under each Unit carries team identity, health bars are side-coloured and appear only on damage, and there are no floating damage numbers. Both decisions serve one goal — a 30-unit Clash must read at one stable scale without per-unit material cost, since #4's asset stack is one texture atlas per pack and every readability target was measured at ~48 px/m.

The costs are accepted, not hidden: the camera cannot watch both ends of the Lane at once, so panning and the *front* hotkey are how you look elsewhere; the desaturated atlas is a one-way pass on shared textures; and without damage numbers an upgrade's exact effect is visible only in the menu. A 0.9 m ring at 1 cm above the ground reads at 86 px tall for one draw call, which is what makes the trade worth taking.

**Considered options:** orthographic top-down (exact pixel scale, but no parallax, so stacked units merge into a flat wall and the 3D models read as sprites); a free camera with zoom and yaw (every readability target and UI proportion would move with it, and pan-zooming mid-Clash loses the fight); always-on auto-follow to the Clash (the camera decides what is watched — kept only as a 2 s-idle convenience that any manual pan cancels); model recolouring for sides (a material instance per unit breaks the single-atlas budget and fights the packs' own palettes); floating damage numbers (a wall of text at 100 units, carrying information the hit flash already gives). The full presentation spec lives in #9.
