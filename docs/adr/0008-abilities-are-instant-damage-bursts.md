# Abilities are automatic, instant damage bursts — no statuses, buffs or heals

Each class's researched **Ability** resolves as damage inside a single tick: a burst at the caster's body, at its target's body, or at a landing point. There is no crowd control, no damage-over-time, no aura, no heal and no damage modifier, and the numbers are fixed constants on the research node that nothing scales. A status effect is not an event: it is per-**Unit** state that must survive in the simulation snapshot across ticks, be duration-ticked, be honoured by the movement layer the engine owns (#10), and be drawn without floating numbers or a second tell (#9) — so the whole feature would be paid for on every tick of every **Match** to buy three abilities of which the player controls none.

**Considered options:** one status effect, the mage's splash slowing what it hits (the most thematic single addition, and the one that most needs a new speed-modifier channel from simulation to movement); the full vocabulary — self-heals, damage reduction, stuns (each a new subsystem and, for damage reduction, an effect that is invisible under #9's ban on damage numbers); buffs on the **Base** menu (the player's only lever would start issuing unit-level effects, which is the control the design deliberately withholds).

**Consequences:** an **Ability** is one `AbilityTriggered` event on the per-tick list, drawn with the splash-ring template in its own accent colour, so #10's seam needs no new state; ability power multiplies by how many **Units** of that class are alive, because **Research** is bought once for the whole class, which is why per-**Unit** ability numbers are small and the composition read is "how much of this class do I field"; every future ability must be expressible as a burst, and anything that needs to persist after the tick that fired it is a new ADR, not a new ability; and the three abilities cannot be balanced by scaling them against **Upgrade levels**, since #8 keeps the two systems orthogonal.
