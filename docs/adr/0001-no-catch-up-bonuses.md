# No catch-up bonuses; comeback is structural

Match flow (#5) explicitly rejected rubber-banding — no bonus income for the side that is behind, no scaled bounties, no "you're losing" gold. The pull toward it is real: with identical income, a player who is losing fights stays behind on units *and* gold, so a losing match can feel sealed at the first bad engagement.

We chose structural comeback levers instead, because a hidden income advantage makes the player's own reading of the game untrustworthy — you cannot tell whether you won a fight or the game decided you should. The four levers are visible and playable: identical income for both sides (no map objectives to fight over), a kill bounty worth only a fraction of the killed unit's cost, an 8-unit army cap that absorbs surplus gold instead of compounding it into mass (15 when this was written; #5 settled it at 8 after #19's dry run), and the defender's shorter reinforcement distance plus base fire. Comeback is therefore a thing the losing player *does* — hold at home, spend on upgrades — rather than a deficit the game silently pays.

**Considered options:** catch-up income scaled to the gold/army gap, and a bounty multiplier for the trailing side. Both were rejected for being invisible to the player and for flattening the reward for winning a fight, which is the only feedback the game gives — the player never controls a unit.

**Consequences:** a losing position is genuinely bad and recovers only through play, so #11 must keep kill bounty a fraction of cost and #13's AI must not be tuned to "let the player back in". If playtesting (#16) shows comebacks never happen, fix it by widening the defender advantage, not by adding income.
